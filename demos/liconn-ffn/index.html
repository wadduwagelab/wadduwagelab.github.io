Faster neuron tracing

LICONN (Tavakoli et al., Nature 2025) maps brain tissue with expansion light microscopy and traces every neuron with a flood-filling network (FFN). Tracing is the expensive step: one network evaluation costs 81.6 GFLOP, and a single dendrite can take thousands of them. We are looking for a faster method that finds the same neurons on any hardware, not just more GPUs.

  • 19.7 msper tracing step: the authors' model in float32 on a V100 (426 ms on a laptop CPU)
  • 9speed-up ideas tested end to end against the authors' proofread segmentation
  • 2.2×float16 with a float32 output head: passes the 300-seed accuracy rule (confirmation on fresh seeds pending)
  • 1.1×with output bitwise identical so far; about 1.3× is the most that route can give with the float32 network unchanged

How FFN tracing works

and why it is slow

  1. Seed. Start from one point inside a neuron.
  2. Look. The network sees a 33×33×33-voxel window of the image plus its current guess of the object, and refines the guess.
  3. Move. Wherever the guess reaches the window's edge (probability ≥ 0.6), queue a new window 8 voxels further along.
  4. Repeat until nothing is left to explore. One object takes hundreds to thousands of steps, one after another.

Cost = number of steps × cost per step. That gives two levers:

A take fewer steps (skip or postpone moves that add little);
B make each step cheaper (smaller network, lower precision, less overhead).

Each step depends on the ones before it, so the trace is a closed loop. A tiny change early can steer it somewhere else later. That turned out to be the central finding.

How results are judged

fixed before any idea was tried

  • Reference: the authors' released model and settings (google/ffn), reproduced bitwise on CPU and confirmed on GPU.
  • Ground truth: the authors' proofread segmentation of the same tissue (500³-voxel crop, 18×18×24 nm voxels).
  • Benchmark: 12 seeds (axons and dendrites) chosen by a fixed rule.
  • Held-out check: a second crop with 12 new seeds, used only to confirm a winner.
  • 300-seed set: 13 further crops chosen by a fixed rule, 300 fresh seeds.

Tolerance (owner-set)

Over the 300 seeds, against the reference, a faster variant passes only if:

  • at most 3 seeds gain a new merge (precision falls by more than 10 points);
  • at most 3 seeds stop early (recall falls by more than 10 points);
  • mean recall and mean precision each fall by at most 1 point.

This replaced the first rule (every seed within 1%) after we found that tiny numerical changes flip rare merges in both directions. Every number on this page comes from runs whose code and outputs are hash-pinned and re-checked by tests.

Key finding: traces are knife-edge at merge points

float16 changes only 0.006% of the network's per-voxel decisions. On the benchmark that was harmless. On held-out data it pushed two dendrite traces across a thin boundary into a large neighbouring neuron, which the trace then followed for thousands of extra steps.

Finding the fix

300 seeds, then a diagnosis

On the 300-seed rule, float16 failed: not on merges (3, at the limit) but because 10 traces stopped early. To see why, every move decision of 20 traces was logged in both precisions and replayed on identical inputs.

  1. Where moves are decided. FFN moves where a face of its box is confident. 91% of those faces sit on a saturated plateau, with scores around 2.94, far above the 0.6 threshold.
  2. What float16 does there. Rounding to about 3 digits likely makes several plateau voxels tie, so the move goes to a neighbouring voxel. The trace drifts, and some drifted traces end sooner. This is a hypothesis; the two hypotheses fixed in advance (moves falling just below 0.6, a bias against moving) were not supported.
  3. The fix. Keep the network in float16 but compute only its tiny output head in float32. Changed move decisions fell from 84% to 16% of steps, at the same speed.

Where a step's time goes

one float16 step on a V100, 10.2 ms

Only about 40% of the time is the network itself; the rest is bookkeeping around it.

Removing that overhead keeps the arithmetic, and therefore the output, bitwise identical. Calling the network directly already gives 9% faster, identical results in float32 (17.9 vs 19.7 ms). In float32 the network itself takes about 14.7 of the 19.7 ms, so removing all of the overhead around an unchanged float32 network gives at most about 1.3×.

Next

in order

  1. Confirm on fresh seeds

    Run the float32 reference and the float32-head network on 300 new seeds from further crops, under the same rule. Only a pass there makes the 2.2× a claim.

  2. Free speed first

    Fuse the bookkeeping (input assembly, canvas update, threading, copies) into the network call. The output stays identical by construction; the gain is up to about 1.3×.

  3. Then a cheaper network

    Try distilling a smaller student network from the authors' model. Distillation needs no new labels, so the public data is enough. Int8 is an untested idea.

How the research is run

faster, without lowering the bar

Everything above took five days (October 18–22, 2026): reading the paper, reproducing the authors' result, setting up a GPU cluster, testing nine ideas, a held-out check, a 300-seed rule, and a diagnosis that led to the fix. AI agents did the engineering. A structured workflow kept the science honest. Below are numbers and real examples from this project's own logs.

The workflow, for every milestone

On a phone, scroll the chart sideways.

How to read it

  1. The owner sets the goal and the rules. What to achieve, the accuracy tolerance, which data and how much compute.
  2. Claude Code proposes the next milestone and drafts its contract: the pre-registered plan for that step (real example below), filled in from a fixed template.
  3. The owner approves the plan, explained in plain words, or changes it. The owner never writes the contract; they decide what it commits to.
  4. The controller freezes it. The controller is a plain script, not an AI. It records the contract, fingerprints the code and runs the unmodified code as a baseline.
  5. Claude Code does the engineering: code, cluster jobs, analysis.
  6. A second model checks the method from the contract and the original sources only, so it cannot inherit the implementer's assumptions.
  7. The controller re-executes everything in a throw-away copy and hashes the code and outputs. An agent saying "it works" is not evidence.
  8. A third model reviews the change against that evidence. If it fails: at most two repairs, then back to the owner.
  9. The owner makes the call. Adopt, move on, or change the rules. A rule change is logged and applies only going forward.

Why it is reliable

  • Separation of roles: the model that writes the code never grades it.
  • No moving goalposts: the tolerance and tests are frozen, and only the owner can change them.
  • Bounded retries: no "retry until it passes"; failures are kept as results.
  • Traceable numbers: every figure links to hashed code and outputs, and any later edit marks it stale.
  • Human judgement where it matters: on research meaning, not on boilerplate.

  1. Goal: what will be built and measured, and what must not change. The reference code stays byte-identical, so the baseline cannot drift.
  2. Allowed paths: Claude may only edit these folders. Data and other projects are off-limits.
  3. Tests: at least 83 tests must exist and pass. Deleting or weakening a test to get a pass is not possible.
  4. Example: a real script the controller runs in a throw-away copy; its output is fingerprinted.
  5. Context: exactly what the independent checker reads: the design and the original code, not the conversation.
  6. Limits: what the result may not claim (one GPU type, one crop, no held-out check yet) and what is off-limits. The agent later broke “no data download beyond metadata”, and the reviewer caught it (the data-access card).

Then: two independent models flagged the same bug. It was fixed and re-checked before any number from the waste analysis was reported.

Model 3mediummeasurement confound

“Screen accuracy and variant selection use batch 8, while tracing and timing use batch 1. GPU convolution algorithms and floating-point results can depend on batch shape…”

Then: the precision screen was re-run at batch 1. The first runs are kept, labelled “superseded”, not deleted.

Model 2mediumreproducibility

“No — it works on this machine by environment coincidence (no GPU/Metal plugin installed), not because the config update is placed early enough.”

Then: the CPU-only reference was enforced in code, so it reproduces bitwise on any machine, not just this one.

Model 3mediumdata-access scope

“The survey downloads segmentation voxel data despite the task's explicit restriction to metadata reads.”

Then: it broke a limit written in the contract (see item 6). It was flagged as a scope breach and taken to the owner, who reviewed it and approved that read explicitly. The approval is recorded in the write-up.

Model 3mediumoverclaim

“The claim that no residual block can be dropped within tolerance is unsupported. Only block 12 and the combined drop of blocks 12 and 17 received closed-loop benchmarks.”

Then: the conclusion was narrowed to what was actually tested.

Model 3highoverclaim

“The conclusion that early stops are not caused by threshold crossings or movement bias is stronger than these analyses establish.”

Then: the write-up was rewritten. The runs describe how float16 diverges; they do not establish the cause, and the plateau explanation is labelled a hypothesis.

Controllerown bug, caught by design

The float16 diagnosis run picked its control seeds by float16's step counts instead of float32's, so the two runs traced different controls.

Then: the analysis refused to compare mismatched runs. The bug was fixed and both runs were repeated; the first pair is kept, labelled “superseded”.

OwnerControllerdesign, not luck

float16 passed all 12 benchmark seeds at 2.19× per step.

Then: the held-out check, fixed in advance, ran 12 new seeds on a separate crop. Two large false merges appeared, so the 2.2× is not claimed. On the benchmark alone it would have looked like a clean win.
