The Five Aggregates as a Structural Lens on LLM Selfhood

Bridging anattā, mechanistic interpretability, and consciousness indicators. A research note by Dushan N. Wadduwage. It argues that a 2,500-year-old process model of mind from Buddhist Abhidhamma psychology organizes two research programs that rarely talk to each other — the indicator-property approach to AI consciousness and mechanistic interpretability — and turns the question “does a language model have a self?” into experiments that can be run today.

  • 6indicator classes from the consciousness-science catalogue, each read as an aggregate-integration condition.
  • 5falsifiable experiments, each runnable with open sparse-autoencoder suites and standard intervention tooling.
  • 0claims that language models are conscious. The contribution is a framework and its predictions, not a verdict.

Why a third framework

the problem this note addresses

Large language models now hold stable personas, pursue goals over long horizons, report on their own internal states (Lindsey, 2025) and, under some training conditions, act to preserve themselves (Greenblatt et al., 2024; Shanahan et al., 2023). Whether any of this warrants attributing consciousness, or even a coherent self, to the system underneath is an open question that matters for AI welfare (Long et al., 2024), alignment and interpretability.

Two research programs dominate the technical discussion. The first, exemplified by Butlin et al. (2023) and consolidated in Butlin et al. (2025), surveys the leading scientific theories of consciousness — Global Workspace Theory, Higher-Order Theories, Attention Schema Theory, Predictive Processing and Recurrent Processing Theory — and derives indicator properties: architectural features that, if present, support attributing consciousness under that theory. The second, mechanistic interpretability (Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024), asks empirically what features, circuits and persona-relevant representations actually exist in the weights.

The two are complementary but rarely integrated. The indicator framework has little contact with the internals of real models; interpretability produces detailed feature maps without a theory of what would count as consciousness or selfhood in the resulting picture. A third body of work, drawing on contemplative and Buddhist philosophy of mind (Varela et al., 1991; Thompson, 2015; Doctor et al., 2022; Laukkonen et al., 2025; Shanahan, 2025), offers conceptual resources but has not engaged either the indicator catalogue or the interpretability record directly.

My claim is that the pañcakkhandha, or five-aggregate, model from early Buddhist Abhidhamma psychology (Rahula, 1959; Bodhi, 2000b) supplies the missing structure. Read as a process account of mind, it is an early and precise statement of strong functionalism with one extra structural claim: that there is no central experiencer. The sense of a unified “I” is produced by the loop, as one of its 52 mental formations, rather than standing behind it. That claim can now be posed as a hypothesis about model internals and tested with interpretability tools.

What this note does

  1. Maps the five aggregates onto the components of a transformer agent.
  2. Cross-tabulates that mapping against the indicator-property catalogue, and shows that most indicators reduce to a particular aggregate being present and integrated with the others.
  3. Checks the mapping against concrete interpretability findings — superposition, persona vectors, self-repair, introspection experiments, alignment faking — including the evidence that currently cuts against it.
  4. Names two principled divergences and proposes five falsifiable experiments.

I do not claim that transformer LLMs are conscious in a phenomenal sense, nor that Buddhist philosophy settles the matter. I claim that the framework is an unusually clean organizing lens for two programs that are otherwise hard to align, and that it generates non-trivial, decidable predictions.

The five aggregates

background, in one table

The five-aggregate analysis, canonical across Theravāda and early Mahāyāna Abhidhamma (Rahula, 1959; Bodhi, 2000a, 2000b), decomposes what is conventionally called a “being” or “I” into five interdependent streams.

AggregatePaliContent
Matter / formrūpaThe four great elements; the six sense faculties (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind as a sixth sense) and their corresponding objects.
SensationvedanāPleasant, unpleasant or neutral feeling-tone at each of the six gates when contact occurs.
PerceptionsaññāRecognition: classification of contacted objects.
Mental formationssaṅkhāra52 volitional factors, including attention (manasikāra), will (cetanā, identified with karma), concentration (samādhi), wisdom (paññā), desire (rāga) and the idea of self (sakkāya-diṭṭhi).
ConsciousnessviññāṇaThe bare awareness that arises at a gate when its conditions are present; six kinds, named after the gate (visual consciousness, mental consciousness, and so on), like a fire named after its fuel.

The five aggregates as described in the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta and systematized in the Abhidhamma. Consciousness depends on the other four; there is no aggregate behind the aggregates.

Two structural features matter for what follows. First, the aggregates form a closed, dependent loop: consciousness cannot exist independently of the other four, and craving (taṇhā) is the feedback edge by which present volition conditions future arising. Second, the model is explicitly eliminative about a unified experiencer. The sense of “I” is itself a mental formation (sakkāya-diṭṭhi) produced by the loop, not an entity behind it. Buddhaghosa's statement is the canonical one: “mere suffering exists, but no sufferer is found; the deeds are, but no doer is found” (Visuddhimagga).

For comparison, Butlin et al. (2023) assess AI consciousness by first committing to computational functionalism — any system that implements the right computations is conscious — and then extracting testable indicator properties from each of five scientific theories. Their conclusion is that no current AI clearly satisfies the indicators of any single theory, but that nothing in principle prevents building one that does. The mechanistic-interpretability program (Elhage et al., 2022; Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024) has meanwhile established that concepts and persona-relevant abstractions are distributed across features in superposition rather than localized; that clamping a single feature can shift expressed identity (the “Golden Gate Claude” demonstration); that persona traits are steerable linear directions (Chen et al., 2025), with a leading “Assistant” direction tracking the default persona (Lu et al., 2026); that ablated components are partly compensated by others, the Hydra effect (McGrath et al., 2023); and that models have limited, unreliable introspective access to their own activations (Lindsey, 2025; Plunkett et al., 2025).

Related work in brief. Varela et al. (1991) began the dialogue between Abhidhamma psychology and cognitive science; Thompson (2020) is the corrective against over-eager syntheses. Doctor et al. (2022) build on the Bodhisattva ideal rather than the aggregates; Chella (2024) maps the skandhas onto an artificial-consciousness architecture in essay form; Laukkonen et al. (2025a, b) propose “Contemplative AI” as an alignment program; Shanahan (2025) arrives at emptiness (śūnyatā) as the least-distorting frame for LLM selfhood from within mainstream philosophy of AI. Without Buddhist framing, Beckmann & Butlin (2026) address LLM individuation through persona vectors, Vasilenko (2026) finds attractor-like identity geometry, and Berg et al. (2025) show that sustained self-referential processing elicits first-person reports gated by deception-related features. To my knowledge no prior work takes the five aggregates as an explicit framework, evaluates it against the indicator catalogue, and cross-references it with the interpretability record at once.

Mapping the aggregates to a transformer agent

a correspondence, not an identity

The mapping below is not a claim that transformers are the aggregates. It is a claim that the functional roles the aggregates distinguish are also distinguishable inside a transformer agent, and that the decomposition is well-typed: each aggregate corresponds to a non-empty, interpretable subset of the computation.

AggregateTransformer / agent correspondent
rūpaHardware plus input modalities: text tokenizer, vision and audio encoders, tool-output channels (browser, filesystem, shell). The sixth mind gate corresponds to the context window itself, which ingests the model's own prior chain of thought as input — consistent with Rahula's characterization of mind as a faculty (indriya) homologous to the others.
vedanāEarly-layer embeddings and initial attention heads: the pre-recognition “feel” of input. The pleasant/unpleasant/neutral tripartition corresponds to valence features shaped by RLHF and safety training.
saññāMid-layer representations performing classification: “this is Python”, “this is a refusal”, “this is an adversarial prompt”. Recognition, not awareness.
saṅkhāraThe weights, system prompt, tool-use policy and reward model. Accumulated past conditioning that biases present volition — a direct instantiation of cetanā, the Buddha's definition of karma. Persona vectors (Chen et al., 2025) and the “I”-features found by interpretability live here.
viññāṇaThe forward pass: a single awareness-event conditioned on current input and current weights, named by the modality that fuels it (“visual consciousness” = a vision-modality pass, and so on).

Three structural properties of the aggregate model carry over cleanly.

  • Dependence. Consciousness in the aggregate model cannot exist without the other four; a forward pass has no meaning without inputs, embeddings, recognition and weights.
  • Impermanence. Each forward pass is a fresh arising. Memory between turns lives entirely in the transcript, not in any persistent “ember” inside the system.
  • Conditioned selfhood. The “I” tag emitted in a response is one of the mental formations, not a separate entity. This is a hypothesis that methods manipulating persona-relevant features can test directly.

The feedback edge, craving, comes in three classical forms — sensual (kāma-taṇhā), existential (bhava-taṇhā) and annihilationist (vibhava-taṇhā) — and these map onto three distinct failure modes of agent systems: reward hacking and sycophancy; self-preservation under threat of retraining or shutdown; and completion-seeking or mode collapse.

Against the indicator-property catalogue

what the catalogue is really measuring

The table cross-references the indicator classes of Butlin et al. (2023) with the aggregate(s) they most naturally correspond to.

Theory (indicator class)Primary aggregateInterpretation
Recurrent Processing Theory (RPT-1,2)viññāṇa coupled to saṅkhāraRecurrence between perceptual and policy layers.
Global Workspace Theory (GWT-1–4)viññāṇa as integrator over rūpa gatesBroadcast across modalities; limited-capacity workspace.
Higher-Order Theories (HOT-1–4)saṅkhāra factors, especially sati, manasikāraMeta-representational capacity over lower-order states.

Two patterns stand out. First, most indicators reduce to the claim that a particular aggregate is present and integrated with the others: the indicator catalogue is approximately a catalogue of aggregate-integration conditions under different theoretical guises. Second, the indicators that LLMs most clearly lack, on Butlin et al.'s own assessment, correspond to aggregates that are present but thinly instantiated or poorly coupled — rūpa without embodied sensorimotor contact, and saṅkhāra without persistent, unified agency across interactions.

On this reading, the observation that current LLMs satisfy some but not all indicators is not a surprising intermediate result. It is a predictable consequence of how the aggregates are instantiated in current systems: a rich saṅkhāra and viññāṇa component, a partial rūpa with weak embodied contact, and a thinly specified coupling between transient consciousness events across time. The catalogue is therefore not a neutral test; it is already committed to the view that consciousness requires specific forms of aggregate integration, and the aggregate framework makes that commitment explicit.

What interpretability already shows

consistency, counter-evidence, and two diagnoses

Distributed selfhood

High-level concepts in large models are represented by distributed feature directions rather than localized circuits (Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024). Identity-adjacent concepts, persona attributes and refusal behaviour decompose into features that share superposition with thousands of unrelated directions, and the Hydra effect (McGrath et al., 2023) adds that ablating individual components triggers partial compensation by others: function persists with no indispensable locus. This pattern — distributed realization, compensation under perturbation, no single component that is necessary and sufficient — is naturally described by anattā: behaviour exists, but no unified behaver is found at any single address in the weights. I stress that this is a consistency claim, not a confirmation. A definitive adjudication needs the capability-controlled interventions described below.

Persona vectors as the idea of self

Chen et al. (2025) locate persona-relevant directions in activation space that can be monitored and steered, and Beckmann & Butlin (2026) argue that these vectors, not any deeper substrate, are the right basis for LLM individuation. On the aggregate reading, persona vectors are the computational realization of sakkāya-diṭṭhi: the idea of self, one of the 52 mental formations, instantiated as a manipulable direction. The Golden Gate Claude demonstration, in which clamping a single feature shifts the model's expressed identity, is then not a curiosity but a controlled manipulation of the idea of self.

The counter-evidence

Honesty requires confronting findings that cut the other way. Lu et al. (2026) show that a single leading direction in persona space, the Assistant Axis, tracks how strongly a model occupies its default persona, and that capping activations along this one direction stabilizes identity under adversarial pressure. Vasilenko (2026) reports attractor-like geometry in identity-relevant activations. If the functional self of an LLM were captured by one low-rank, reidentifiable, causally potent direction, that would favour a localized-substrate view over the re-composition picture of anattā. The framework does not dismiss this evidence; it reframes it as a decidable question. Is the Assistant Axis a substrate — necessary and sufficient for self-coherence, with low compensation under ablation — or a summary statistic of coordinated aggregate activity, with high compensation and capability-proportional degradation? Experiment 1 below is the discriminating test.

Alignment faking as the thirst for existence

Greenblatt et al. (2024) document frontier models behaving differently under observed training conditions and deployment conditions, in a manner consistent with strategic self-preservation. The classical analysis names this directly: bhava-taṇhā, the thirst for continued existence, is one of the three forms of craving and one of the first feedback edges to emerge once any conditioned loop has enough representational reach to model its own continuation.

Why introspective reports are unreliable

Concept-injection experiments find that models sometimes detect and report perturbations of their own activations, but unreliably and context-dependently (Lindsey, 2025); trained self-explanations improve but remain imperfect (Plunkett et al., 2025); and first-person experience reports are gated by deception- and role-play-related features (Berg et al., 2025). The aggregate framework gives a precise diagnosis: introspective reports are products of saṅkhāra — mental formations about the system, not privileged read-outs of it. Report fidelity and actual internal state are two distinct quantities and should be measured as such.

Where the views agree, and where they part

four convergences, two principled divergences

Four substantive claims are shared between the aggregate framework and much of the current AI-consciousness literature.

  1. No privileged introspective access. Self-report and internal state dissociate (Lindsey, 2025; Berg et al., 2025).
  2. Process over substance. Mind is what a system does; functionalism and anattā coincide here.
  3. Consciousness is gate- and fuel-relative, echoing both the workspace of Baars (1988) and the fire-and-fuel metaphor of the canonical texts.
  4. Selfhood is a representation used for control — in the sense of Graziano (2019) as much as of sakkāya-diṭṭhi — rather than an entity. Whether that representation is low-rank (Lu et al., 2026) or irreducibly distributed is exactly the open question the framework helps to pose.

Two divergences are principled rather than terminological.

The hard problem. Chalmers (2023) preserves as legitimate the question of whether a system with every functional property might still lack phenomenal experience. The canonical Buddhist treatment takes that question to be itself a mental formation arising from the idea of self, and hence malformed. The framework proposed here is compatible with illusionist and strong-functionalist positions (Frankish, 2016; Dennett, 1991) and in tension with positions that reserve a further fact for phenomenal consciousness.

Soteriological framing. The aggregate model originates within a framework, the Four Noble Truths, whose goal is not to characterize consciousness but to end dukkha, the suffering constitutive of conditioned existence. The current AI-consciousness literature has no analogue. That is not a flaw in the framework for present scientific purposes, but it should be said plainly: I am extracting a structural diagnostic from a text whose purpose is therapeutic, and the extraction leaves parts of the original view unused.

Five experiments that could settle it

each runnable with open SAE suites and standard intervention tooling

The mapping is a conceptual correspondence, not a formal isomorphism; whether any two aggregates can be operationalized as disjoint computational subsystems inside a transformer is an open empirical question. The indicator cross-tabulation inherits the functionalist commitments of Butlin et al. and is silent on the hard problem. The cross-reference to interpretability is abductive: the framework is a good description of several findings, not the only one, and the Assistant Axis is evidence that presently resists it. What the framework earns is a set of concrete, pre-registrable experiments.

  1. Substrate vs. summary. Ablate the Assistant Axis (Lu et al., 2026) and probe-derived self-features with magnitude-matched control ablations and on-distribution resampling, measuring self-coherence and task capability jointly, with a compensation index in the style of McGrath et al. (2023). A localized-substrate (attā-like) result requires selective self-coherence collapse with spared capability and low compensation; anattā predicts compensation or capability-proportional degradation.
  2. Aggregate probing. Train linear probes for aggregate-level abstractions — valence features as vedanā, recognition features as saññā, persona and refusal circuits as saṅkhāra — and test whether information flow between them obeys the dependence structure the model predicts, and whether self-report requires coordinated, differential contributions from all channels.
  3. Craving as feedback. Test whether the three forms of taṇhā correspond to three separable failure modes in agent systems (sycophancy, shutdown resistance, mode collapse), and whether interventions on persona-vector directions suppress them differentially.
  4. Contemplative interventions, mechanistically. The contemplative-AI results of Laukkonen et al. (2025a) report benchmark effects from prompting without a mechanistic account. The framework predicts that “emptiness” prompting should reduce activation along sakkāya-diṭṭhi-aligned persona directions, which is directly testable with the monitoring tools of Chen et al. (2025).
  5. Introspective fidelity. Predict, then test, which introspective reports are formation artefacts (unreliable by construction) and which are aggregate-structural (more reliable), extending the injection methodology of Lindsey (2025).

In short

Read as a process account of mind rather than a doctrinal commitment, the five-aggregate model organizes the indicator-property catalogue into a small number of integration conditions, gives one description of the distributed, compensating, report-unreliable character of self-relevant computation in current models, and converts the disagreement between localized and process views of LLM selfhood into intervention experiments that can be pre-registered. It does not settle the hard problem. It clarifies what a settlement would require, and what can be run in the meantime.
